[Concept_Explanation_Template_1.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Pressure Is Force per Area
NARRATION-0 = Pressure tells us how strongly a force is spread over a surface.
DEFINITION = Pressure is the force acting perpendicular on a surface, divided by the area of that surface.
NARRATION-1 = So the same pressure produces a larger force on a larger area.
FORMULA = p = \frac{F}{A} \qquad \Leftrightarrow \qquad F = p \cdot A
SYMBOL-1 = p
MEANING-1 = pressure
UNIT-1 = Pa = N/m²
SYMBOL-2 = F
MEANING-2 = force perpendicular to the surface
UNIT-2 = N
SYMBOL-3 = A
MEANING-3 = area
UNIT-3 = m²
EX-TITLE = Worked Example: Your Hand
NARRATION-EX-0 = How strongly does the air press on your open hand?
EX-PROBLEM = An open hand has an area of about 0.01 m². How large is the force of the air on it (p₀ = 101 325 Pa)?
NARRATION-EX-1 = First, we solve the formula for the force.
EX-STEP-1 = $F = p_0 \cdot A$
NARRATION-EX-2 = Then we insert the values.
EX-STEP-2 = $F = 101\,325\ \text{Pa} \cdot 0.01\ \text{m}^2$
NARRATION-EX-3 = That is about the weight of a hundred kilograms – and you do not feel it, because the air presses from both sides.
EX-STEP-3 = $F \approx 1013\ \text{N}$ – the weight of about 100 kg
NARRATION-EX-4 = Now it is your turn.
TASK = A table top measures 1.2 m × 0.8 m. How large is the force of the air on it, in kN?
RESULT-VALUE = 97.27
HINT-1 = First calculate the area A of the table top.
HINT-2 = F = 101 325 Pa · 0.96 m², then divide by 1000.
SOLUTION = A = 1.2 m · 0.8 m = 0.96 m² and F = 101 325 Pa · 0.96 m² ≈ 97 272 N ≈ 97.3 kN. The table does not break, because the air pushes from below with the same force.

[Application_Transfer_Template_2.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Spot the Mistake
NARRATION-0 = A student calculated the force on Guericke's hemispheres. One of the four steps is wrong.
PROBLEM = Calculate the force that holds the hemispheres together (diameter 50 cm, perfect vacuum inside, p₀ = 101 325 Pa), in kN.
STEP-1 = $F = p_0 \cdot A$
STEP-2 = $A = \pi r^2$
STEP-3 = $A = \pi \cdot (0.50\ \text{m})^2 = 0.785\ \text{m}^2$
STEP-4 = $F = 101\,325\ \text{Pa} \cdot 0.785\ \text{m}^2 \approx 79.6\ \text{kN}$
NARRATION-1 = Which step is wrong?
LOCATE-HINT = Compare the length used in step 3 with the data given in the task.
LOCATE-FEEDBACK = Step 3 uses the diameter (0.50 m) instead of the radius (0.25 m), so the area – and the force – come out four times too large.
NARRATION-2 = Now give the correct result.
CORRECT-QUESTION = What is the correct force in kN?
CORRECT-ANSWER = 19.9
CORRECT-HINT = Repeat step 3 with r = 0.25 m.
SOLUTION = r = 0.25 m, A = π · (0.25 m)² ≈ 0.196 m², F = 101 325 Pa · 0.196 m² ≈ 19.9 kN – about the weight of two tonnes.


[Simulation_Template_3.md]
SLIDE-TITLE = Air Pressure and Altitude
NARRATION-0 = The higher we go, the less air lies above us. This program calculates the air pressure at different altitudes.
CODE-INTRO = The program uses the barometric formula p = p₀ · e^(−h/H) with a scale height H of about 8.4 km.
PY-CODE = """
import math

p0 = 101325     # pressure at sea level in Pa
H = 8400        # scale height of the atmosphere in m

for h in range(0, 11000, 1000):
    p = p0 * math.exp(-h / H)
    print(f"{h:6d} m: {p / 1000:6.1f} kPa")
"""
NARRATION-1 = Run the program and read the table.
EXPLORE-TASK = At which altitude in the table (in m) does the pressure first drop below half of the sea-level value?
EXPLORE-ANSWER = 6000
EXPLORE-HINT = Half of 101.3 kPa is about 50.7 kPa.
EXPLORE-FEEDBACK = At 6000 m the pressure is only 49.6 kPa – mountaineers feel this as "thin air".
